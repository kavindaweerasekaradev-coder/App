ReadyMe
Redesigning a learning app end to end

ReadyMe helps Sri Lankan students prepare for their G.C.E. A/L exams through structured learning paths built by tutors. I led a complete redesign of the live app, from the information architecture and flows to every screen and a new design system, turning a dense, subject-first app into a calm, discovery-led learning experience.

At a glance

Same product, same brand colour. A completely new experience.

The problem

The app was organised around a fixed list of exam subjects. Students landed on near-identical cards, a permanent subscribe banner and no clear next step.

My approach

I audited every flow, rebuilt the navigation around learning, and designed a component system so every screen shares the same patterns and hierarchy.

What changed

A discovery-first home, a clear course hub, a calmer question flow and one consistent visual language across iOS, iPad and web.

Who it's for

Two audiences, one learning loop.

Students

Preparing for their G.C.E. A/L exams on the ReadyMe app and web.

What they need

  • Find the right learning path and tutor quickly
  • Practise exam-style questions without distractions
  • See progress and know what to do next
Tutors

Build and publish learning paths and question sets in the authoring tool.

What they need

  • Have their content presented clearly and credibly
  • Reach the right students
  • Show ratings, language and subject at a glance

UX audit

Where the old app got in the way. Hover a number to see the issue.

  1. 1

    Subject-first entry

    Home opened on a fixed list of exam subjects, so there was nothing to discover and no sense of what to do next.

  2. 2

    Dense, all-caps metadata

    Exam name, provider and plan were squeezed into small all-caps lines that were hard to scan.

  3. 3

    Repetitive cards

    Every subject card repeated the same two counters, so nothing stood out or invited a tap.

  4. 4

    Floating button over content

    A floating action button sat on top of the list and covered part of a card.

  5. 5

    Paywall before value

    A subscribe banner was pinned to the home screen before students had experienced any content.

  6. 6

    Navigation not built for learning

    One of three main tabs went to Settings, while finding and continuing learning had no clear home.

Design principles

Three rules for every decision.

01

Learning first, paywall second

Lead with content students want, and ask for a subscription only once they've seen its value.

02

One job per screen

Clear hierarchy, generous whitespace and a single obvious primary action on every screen.

03

Built as a system

Tokens and reusable components, so every flow feels like the same product and ships faster.

Before → after

Seven flows, redesigned. Drag to compare.

01

Home from subject list to discovery

  • “My Subjects” list replaced by a Discover feed: latest learning paths, featured tutors and exam-prep rows
  • Rich path cards with tutor, rating, language and subject tags
  • Subscribe banner removed from the main view; a four-tab bar (Discover, Activity, My paths, Account) gives clear entry points
02

Choosing what to learn

  • Checkbox list of exam subjects became browsable learning-path cards
  • Each path shows its tutor, rating and tags, so students can compare at a glance
  • Search and “See all” lists use the same card, keeping the system consistent
03

Course page a clear hub

  • Stats tiles and a long topics table replaced by a focused hub
  • Four clear destinations: Learning materials, Analytics, Chat with tutor and Leaderboard
  • “New content” badge highlights what changed since the last visit
04

Answering questions

  • Timer and number strip replaced by a calm progress bar and “Question 5/10”
  • Image-led questions get room to breathe, with Quick ref one tap away
  • Answers move to their own view, toggled from a single bottom control
05

Choosing an answer

  • Boxed A/B/C/D options became full-sentence choices that are easier to read
  • One clear primary action, Submit, instead of a dark Next bar
  • Previous / next and Question toggle stay in the same place on every question
06

Login

  • Warmer welcome and a simpler, left-aligned form
  • Google, Facebook and Apple sign-in as a compact row instead of full-width buttons
  • Clear “Create an account” path at the bottom
07

Plans & paywall

  • Four stacked price boxes replaced by a single, focused “Unlock full access” card
  • The plan choice is the only decision on the screen
  • Copy speaks to the learning path the student is already on

Design system

One system behind every screen.

  • Primary#5A5EDEActions, links, progress
  • Primary tint#EEEEFCSelected states, soft buttons
  • Surface#F6F8FACards and answer options
  • Rating#FEC802Stars and highlights
  • Success tint#E7F7EDLearning materials
  • Warm tint#FEFADCLeaderboard

Foundations

  • Color tokens, typography scale, spacing system and grid
  • Improved type hierarchy for headlines, subheadings and body text
  • Whitespace and contrast used to highlight what matters

Components

  • Reusable cards, buttons, inputs and progress indicators
  • Standardized hover, active and disabled states
  • Clear primary, secondary and tertiary actions

Navigation & content

  • Content grouped into Learning paths, Progress and Recommendations
  • Simplified navigation to reduce decision fatigue
  • Clear entry points and personalized content
