01 · Overview

I redesigned how Sri Lanka orders pizza.

Ordering pizza should be the easiest part of the night. Online, it often isn’t: too many steps before anything reaches the cart, prices that change at checkout, and no simple way to say “ready at 7, please”.

My role

This is my concept for the Domino’s Sri Lanka website. I led the UX and UI end to end, from the first sketches to a working, responsive prototype.

02 · The problem

Hungry people don’t read instructions.

I walked through a typical online pizza order step by step, like a hungry customer would. These thoughts kept coming up.

  1. 01

    Too many steps

    “I have to pick a crust before I can even add it?”

  2. 02

    Price surprises

    “Wait… why is it Rs 600 more now?”

  3. 03

    Unclear timing

    “Which store even delivers here?”

  4. 04

    No order ahead

    “Can I just order it for 7 pm?”

  1. 01

    Add in one tap

    Smart defaults on every pizza. Customise only if you want to.

  2. 02

    No price surprises

    Real prices with taxes, and the best deal applied for you.

  3. 03

    Built for Sri Lanka

    Local areas, stores, wallets and LankaQR.

  4. 04

    Now or later

    Order for right now, or book up to seven days ahead.

03 · Who it’s for

Meet Nethmi. Hungry, and busy.

A proto-persona kept every decision grounded in a real moment: ordering on a phone, between other things, often for a group.

N

Proto-persona and journey based on assumptions and a heuristic review, to be validated with real customers.

04 · Real moments

Designed for real people, in real moments.

Three everyday moments shaped every decision: the team lunch, the late night with friends, and the rider bringing it to the door.

05 · Process

From napkin to pixels.

Every screen went through the same three steps: an idea on paper, structure in grey boxes, then the flavour.

  1. 1Sketch: quick ideas on paper. The big one: order straight from the hero.
  2. 2Wireframe: grey boxes to lock the hierarchy: what, how much, one button.
  3. 3Interface: brand colour, real photos, live prices and motion.

06 · What I improved

My first version worked. It just wasn’t Domino’s.

The first build was clean but generic: illustrated pizzas, a soft beige palette and the same card everywhere. I rebuilt every screen around the brand and the four goals. These are real screens from both versions.

07 · Before and after

From a search banner to a hero you can order from.

The first hero asked people to search. The new one puts a pizza, a size and an Add button on the very first screen.

Home

  • Order from the hero: a swipeable deck of featured pizzas with a size picker and one-tap Add.
  • Brand first: Domino’s blue, real food photos instead of illustrations, and the tile motif.
  • A reason to act now: today’s deal with a live countdown sits right under the headline.

From plain lists to offers that sell

  • Photos lead: full-bleed food photography with the name and price laid over it.
  • Ranked, not listed: big #1, #2, #3 numbers help first-timers pick a favourite fast.
  • Deals you can grab: tear-off coupons with an “ends tonight” timer, applied automatically.

From identical cards to photo-first cards

  • Size on the card: a sliding size switcher, with the real price for that size.
  • Add without a dialog: one tap adds the pizza with sensible defaults. Customise is optional.
  • Filters that stay: category tabs, Veg only, Spicy and search stick while you scroll.

Less to read, more to see

  • See the size: real pizza photos that grow with the inches, instead of small icons.
  • Live total: the Add button always shows what you’ll pay as you choose.
  • Deal applied for you: the cart finds the best deal and spells out the saving. No codes.

From “schedule for later” to real order ahead

  • Progress, not paperwork: a clean progress tracker replaces the stack of numbered cards.
  • Day, then time: seven days ahead, grouped by lunch to late night, with busy times marked Full.
  • A clear commitment: “Book order” shows the exact time and store before you pay.

From a sign-off to a brand moment

  • The promise, big: “Hot. Fresh. Yours.” with a gentle light sweep over an ingredient wall.
  • Still useful: track an order, call the hotline and check opening hours, right there.
  • Every way to pay: cards, cash, Genie, eZ Cash, FriMi and LankaQR, shown with their logos.

08 · Mobile

On the phone, where most orders start.

The same improvements, sized for one hand: bigger photos, actions within thumb reach, and nothing hidden.

09 · User flow

One clear path, from craving to collected.

Every route leads to the same single-page checkout. The only fork is when: right now, or booked for later.

10 · Brand

Hot. Fresh. Yours.

The promise I designed around. Every screen should feel hot (fast, energetic), fresh (clean, honest) and yours (personal, made your way).

  1. 01

    Bold

    Big type, big photos, one big button.

  2. 02

    Warm

    Talks like a friend at the counter.

  3. 03

    Honest

    Real prices and real times, always.

  4. 04

    Quick

    Nothing between you and pizza.

11 · Visual identity

Unmistakably Domino’s, dialled up.

Only the brand’s own colours, the domino tile as a quiet motif, a characterful grotesque for appetite and a calm sans for reading.

Typography

  • Bricolage Grotesque · headlines and prices
  • Plus Jakarta Sans · body, labels and UI

12 · Art direction

Warm photos, friendly people.

Food is shot close and real, never glossy. People are drawn by hand in simple line art, so the brand feels human without stock-photo smiles.

13 · Components

A small kit that does a lot.

One strong button for the action that matters, segmented pickers for sizes, honest tags for diet and heat, and one icon set at a single stroke weight.

14 · Key screens

A hero you can order from.

The first screen is a swipeable deck of pizzas with a size picker and a one-tap Add, followed by offers that sell themselves.

Home

  • Swipe, tap or use the arrow keys
  • Live price per size, under the photo
  • Campaign posters inspired by the shopfronts

Filter in a tap, customise in a sheet

  • Veg, Spicy and search always in reach
  • The pizza photo grows with the size
  • Running total on the Add button

A cart that does the maths

  • Delivery or pickup, switched at the top
  • Free-delivery progress, no guessing
  • Taxes included in every price

Now, or order ahead

  • Day, then time, grouped by lunch, afternoon, evening and late night
  • Busy times are marked Full instead of hidden
  • Change or cancel until 30 minutes before

Pay the local way, track to the door

  • Cards with 3-D Secure, Genie, eZ Cash, FriMi and LankaQR
  • The card preview updates as you type
  • Clear stages from oven to door
  • Change time or cancel, right from tracking

15 · Responsive

From a 320px phone to a 4K screen.

The same components reflow across twelve tested screen sizes, with no sideways scrolling anywhere.

16 · Outcome

What the redesign delivers.

A working, responsive prototype that removes the biggest points of friction, ready to test with real customers.

  1. 01

    1 tap

    To add a pizza, from the hero or any menu card.

  2. 02

    1 page

    Checkout: details, timing and payment together.

  3. 03

    7 days

    Order ahead: pickup or delivery, changeable later.

  4. 04

    5

    Local ways to pay: cards, Genie, eZ Cash, FriMi, LankaQR.

  5. 05

    320px up to 4K

    Twelve screen sizes, no sideways scroll.

  6. 06

    AA · WCAG 2.2

    Keyboard, screen reader and reduced motion.

What I learned

  • Trust is designed in the details. Showing the real price early did more than any visual flourish.

What I’d test next

  • Usability sessions with customers in Colombo, and the hero deck against a menu-first home.

What comes after

  • Reorder in one tap, group orders, and loyalty points shown right in the cart.
